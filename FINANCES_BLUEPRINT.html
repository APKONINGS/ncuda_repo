NCUDA Member Portal · Finance blueprint

How money moves through NCUDA

Members pay in person to their chapter's Financial Secretary. The portal does not take payments. It keeps the record: who paid what, for what, when, and who received it, with a receipt number every time.

2 · Money coming in

Three kinds of payment, always paid in full

There are no part-payments. Every payment is the full amount due, so a record is either paid or not paid.

Registration

Being an active member

  • Each chapter's Financial Secretary sets one fee for everyone in the chapter, officers included.
  • Paid when a person is registered, and again each year when the President General starts the yearly renewal.
  • Until it is recorded, the person's account is inactive.
Contributions

Paying towards a project

  • A project has a purpose and two amounts: one for executives, one for members.
  • Everyone covered by the project pays their amount once.
  • Members see what they still owe under My Summary.
Fines

Penalties with a reason

  • The Financial Secretary records the reason and the amount.
  • Paid on the spot, or recorded as owed and paid later in one payment.
  • Unpaid fines must be cleared before a member can move to another chapter.

4 · Money going out

Two ways money leaves a fund

Expenses

Spending by the keeper of the funds

  • Chapter funds: the chapter's Financial Secretary.
  • Regional funds: the Regional Coordinator.
  • Central funds: the National Financial Secretary.
  • Each expense gets a voucher number, such as PV-2026-00007.
Project money

Funding a committee's work

  • Committees take no payments themselves. Members always pay their chapter.
  • For a national or regional project, the chapter sends the money to the project manager, whether or not the project has a committee: the National Financial Secretary in office for a national project, the region's Regional Coordinator in office for a regional one. It is recorded as a chapter expense, and the project manager gets a receipt in their notifications.
  • A chapter project's money stays in the chapter.
  • If the project has committees, the project manager gives them what they need (for a chapter project, the chapter's Financial Secretary does, from the chapter's funds). The committee's Financial Secretary gets a receipt too.
MembersPay their contribution

To their chapter's Financial Secretary.

→
Chapter Financial SecretarySends project money

Never more than the chapter collected for that project and has not already sent.

→
Project managerReceives and holds it

Always the post, not the person: the National Financial Secretary in office (national projects) or the region's Regional Coordinator in office (regional ones).

→
CommitteeReceives what it needs

Never more than the project manager holds. The committee delivers the result.

5 · Safeguards

How the records stay trustworthy

  • Amounts are set, not typedThe portal fills in the full amount due from the project, the fine or the chapter's fee.
  • Numbers are automaticReceipt and voucher numbers are generated by the portal and cannot be chosen.
  • The payer is always toldEvery payment sends its receipt to the payer's notifications; a fine imposed or a record voided is reported to them too, with the reason.
  • Nothing is deletedA wrong entry is voided with a written reason and stays in the history.
  • Everything is loggedThe audit log shows who did what and when, before and after, including who viewed or downloaded financial records.
  • Limits on sending moneyChapters cannot send more than they collected; project managers cannot give more than they hold.
  • The right person recordsOnly a chapter's own Financial Secretary records its payments. Others may look but not change.

Officers also see each person's phone and email beside their name, so they can follow up directly.

Everything an officer can see can also be printed (records, expenses, registration and the summary) or downloaded as a spreadsheet for the books. A download holds only what that officer may already see, and each one is recorded in the audit log.
